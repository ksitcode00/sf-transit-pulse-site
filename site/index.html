    How we build each result

    What we check before recommending a trip.

    SF Transit Pulse answers one practical question: What should I take right now, and why?

    We only say what the available data supports.

    1. 1
      Collect current updates

      Check vehicle locations, arrival estimates, service notices, route paths, and when each source was updated.

    2. 2
      Check each route direction

      Look for steady vehicle spacing, vehicles too close together, long waits, and limited live data in each direction.

    3. 3
      Build trips you may be able to make

      Compare direct and one-transfer trips. When complete live predictions are available, check whether two specific trips connect; otherwise label the time as an estimate.

    4. 4
      Compare what matters to you

      Fastest favors transit time. Balanced weighs time, steady service, walking, transfers, and only large historical differences. Historical context gives those past differences more weight.

    5. 5
      Explain the recommendation

      Show why one route ranks first, what the other options offer, and where the data is limited.

    Important limits

    • Historical incident data cannot tell whether you will be safe.
    • A nearby street event does not prove what caused a transit delay.
    • No live update does not mean a route has stopped running.
    • A comparison score is not an arrival time.
    • Live predictions can change, so a possible transfer is not guaranteed.
